Fixed-Budget Comparison Analysis (n=100, budget=100k evals, 10 runs)

Algorithms: Random Search (RS), RLS, (1+1) EA, MMAS (ρ ∈ {1, 1/√n, 1/n})

---------------------------------------------------------------------
F1 (OneMax)
- Ordering: RLS ≈ (1+1) EA ≈ MMAS  ≫ RS
- Fast early convergence; RS slow.
- MMAS ρ=1/√n slightly more stable.

F2 (LeadingOnes)
- Ordering: RLS ≳ (1+1) EA ≳ MMAS ≫ RS
- One-bit improvements suit RLS; (1+1) EA close; MMAS stable but slower.

F3 (Linear)
- Ordering: RLS ≈ (1+1) EA ≈ MMAS ≫ RS
- All converge quickly; RS far behind.

F18 (LABS)
- Ordering: MMAS(ρ=1/√n or 1/n) ≳ (1+1) EA ≥ RLS ≫ RS
- MMAS keeps improving where locals plateau.

F23 (N-Queens)
- Ordering: MMAS ≳ (1+1) EA ≥ RLS ≫ RS
- MMAS more stable; RLS noisy, often regresses.

F24 (Concatenated Trap)
- Ordering: MMAS(ρ≤1/√n) > (1+1) EA ≥ RLS ≫ RS
- MMAS escapes traps; RLS/(1+1)EA stagnate.

F25 (NK-Landscape)
- Ordering: MMAS ≳ (1+1) EA ≥ RLS ≫ RS
- MMAS best robustness; exploration advantage on rugged landscape.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Cross-Function Takeaways:
- RS = baseline only.
- RLS good for smooth functions (F1–F3), weak on deceptive ones.
- (1+1) EA improves robustness slightly.
- MMAS best overall; balances speed and exploration.

ρ Parameter:
- ρ=1 fast but may overfit early.
- ρ=1/√n or ρ=1/n yields steadier, longer-term gains.
- Recommended default: ρ=1/√n.

Conclusion:
MMAS achieves near-optimal performance on easy functions and superior performance on deceptive/rugged ones (F24, F25, F18). RLS and (1+1)EA remain strong baselines but lack global exploration. RS confirms baseline difficulty.
