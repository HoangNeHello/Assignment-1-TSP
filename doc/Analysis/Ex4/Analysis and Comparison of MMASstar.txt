This exercise implemented the MMAS and MMAS* algorithms with three evaporation rates ρ ∈ {1, 1/√n, 1/n}, and compared their performance against Random Search, RLS, and (1+1) EA from Exercise 2. 
All experiments were executed for 100,000 evaluations on the benchmark functions F1, F2, F3, F18, F23, F24, and F25. The fixed-budget plots illustrate the mean and standard deviation of the best-so-far fitness across ten independent runs.

Overall, the results demonstrate that MMAS* significantly outperforms Random Search and RLS on most functions, and achieves comparable or superior performance to (1+1) EA depending on the problem characteristics. 
Specifically, MMAS* with ρ = 1/n consistently shows the most stable convergence across unimodal and plateau functions, while ρ = 1 leads to faster early improvements but slightly higher variance. 
The variant ρ = 1/√n typically achieves a balance between convergence speed and stability.

Function F1–F3:
The three unimodal problems confirm theoretical expectations from Exercise 2. 
Both RLS and (1+1) EA converge efficiently with O(n log n) evaluations, while MMAS* reaches similar optima after slightly more iterations.
For F1 and F2, (1+1) EA and MMAS*(ρ=1/n) perform almost identically in late stages, supporting the known efficiency of elitist mutation-based and pheromone-based optimization methods on smooth fitness landscapes.

Function F18, F23, F24:
These problems exhibit multiple local optima or plateaus. Here, MMAS* clearly outperforms the (1+1) EA and RLS. The pheromone update mechanism allows MMAS* to maintain useful search diversity, preventing premature convergence. 
Among the variants, MMAS*(ρ=1/√n) and MMAS*(ρ=1/n) show the most consistent progress, as excessive evaporation (ρ=1) causes unstable search behavior in these rugged landscapes.

Function F25:
This problem is designed to test the ability to escape local optima. The MMAS* variants quickly converge to near-optimal solutions, with ρ=1/n again demonstrating the most stable performance.
RLS and Random Search fail to improve significantly after early iterations, confirming the advantage of ACO-style probabilistic exploration on non-smooth fitness surfaces.

In conclusion:
MMAS* provides an effective trade-off between exploration and exploitation through its adaptive pheromone learning mechanism.
Compared to MMAS, which updates pheromones using the iteration-best solution, MMAS* leverages the best-so-far solution for reinforcement.
This subtle change enables MMAS* to preserve useful global information and achieve smoother convergence, especially on rugged landscapes.
Compared to the algorithms in Exercise 2, it exhibits better scalability and robustness across both unimodal and multimodal landscapes.
The experimental results empirically support the theoretical analyses from Exercise 2, where the expected runtime of mutation-based algorithms on unimodal functions is O(n log n), while ACO-based algorithms extend this efficiency to more complex problem structures through pheromone reinforcement.
